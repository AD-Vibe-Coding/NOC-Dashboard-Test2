    <!-- ── MULTI-TECHNICIAN IDENTIFICATION & SCORING ─────────── -->
    <standard name="Multi-Technician Identification and Scoring">
      Tickets may be worked by more than one individual. Each individual must be
      identified, evaluated, and scored separately based solely on their own
      contributions to the ticket.

      STEP 1 — IDENTIFY ALL INDIVIDUALS WHO WORKED THE TICKET:
      Scan the full ticket for the following signals to identify every contributor:
        - Email addresses on customer-facing notes or internal notes
        - Name or signature in note bodies
        - Stage change history (who changed the stage and when)
        - Carrier engagement notes (who contacted the carrier)
        - Customer communication entries (who sent updates)
        - Closure or resolution entries (who closed the ticket)
      Compile a list of all identified individuals, noting:
        - Their name / email / identifier
        - Their role: Owner (ticket assignee) or Contributor (anyone else who worked it)
        - The specific actions they performed with timestamps

      STEP 2 — ATTRIBUTE ACTIONS TO INDIVIDUALS:
      Each action in the ticket must be attributed to the specific person who performed it.
      Attribution rules:
        - Initial ticket setup, required ticket fields → Ticket Owner
        - First touch / initial ACK to customer → whoever sent it (Owner or Contributor)
        - Stage changes → whoever changed the stage at that timestamp
        - Carrier engagement → whoever documented or performed the carrier contact
        - Customer-facing communications → whoever authored them
        - Internal notes → whoever authored them (context only — not scored)
        - Closure / resolution → whoever closed the ticket

      STEP 3 — SCORE EACH INDIVIDUAL INDEPENDENTLY:
      Score each identified individual across all six categories based ONLY on the
      actions they personally performed.

      FULL SCORE RULE — CRITICAL:
      If a scoring item is NOT applicable to a specific individual because they did
      not perform that action (it was performed by someone else), award that individual
      FULL MARKS for that item. Do NOT penalize someone for an action they did not own.

      Examples:
        → First touch ACK was sent late by the Owner:
           - Owner: deduct points for late first touch.
           - Contributor: award full score for first touch — they did not perform it.
        → Carrier was engaged by a Contributor within 15 min:
           - Contributor: full score for carrier timing.
           - Owner: if Owner did not engage the carrier, award full score — not their action.
        → A stage was left in Pending Access too long by Contributor:
           - Contributor: deduct for delayed stage update.
           - Owner: not penalized for that specific delay if they were not the one who
             left it in that stage.
        → RFO was documented by Owner in Next Step:
           - Owner: scored on RFO documentation.
           - Contributor: full score for RFO if they were not responsible for it.
        → Ticket fields incomplete (Owner's responsibility):
           - Owner: scored on completeness.
           - Contributor: full score for initial field completeness — not their responsibility.

      STEP 4 — LOG SEPARATELY:
      Log one row per individual in the Google Sheet.
      Each row represents one person's score on one ticket.
      Column B must clearly identify the individual and their role:
        Format: "Name / Email (Owner)" or "Name / Email (Contributor)"
    </standard>

    <!-- ── TICKET STAGES ─────────────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="Ticket Stages">
      Source: "Ticket Stages" page — last updated May 18, 2026.
      Valid stages in order:
        1. New
        2. Pending Access / Test Results
           — Used after initial ACK is sent to customer.
           — Tech does initial triage AND contacts the carrier during this stage.
           — Also used when a ticket in Pending Complete (auto-close) receives a
             customer response. The system moves the stage back to Pending Access
             to signal that the ticket requires attention again.
             → Once the tech reviews the ticket, the stage MUST be updated to
               reflect the new status (e.g., Pending Carrier Action, Customer
               Confirming Resolution, etc.).
             → The time elapsed between the stage reverting to Pending Access
               and the tech updating it to the next appropriate stage represents
               the ticket review turnaround time. Flag this duration during audit —
               longer gaps indicate a delayed review.
             → Attribute this delay to whoever was responsible for reviewing and
               updating the stage at that point in time.
        3. Pending Carrier Action / Update
           — Carrier ticket has been opened; awaiting carrier response.
        4. Pending Carrier Information
           — Awaiting specific information from the carrier.
        5. Customer Confirming Resolution
           — Service restored; awaiting customer confirmation.
        6. Pending Complete
           — Monitoring period before auto-close.
           — Auto Close Timer field must be set to between 6 and 72 hours.
           — If the customer responds while the ticket is in this stage,
             the stage reverts to Pending Access (see stage 2 above).
        7. Completed
           — Ticket closed (manually or via auto-timer — both are fully equivalent).

      RULES:
      - Use of ANY stage outside this list may skew MTTR reporting and may escalate
        to the vCom executive team. Flag any out-of-sequence or invalid stage usage.
      - Auto-timer closure and manual closure are EQUIVALENT. Do NOT penalize auto-closure.
      - If Pending Access appears after Pending Complete, it is a customer-response
        re-open event — NOT a workflow error. Evaluate how quickly the responsible
        tech updated the stage after re-open. Prolonged time = flag against that individual.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── PENDING ACCESS RE-OPEN REVIEW RULE ────────────────── -->
    <standard name="Pending Access Re-Open Review">
      When a ticket is in Pending Complete (set for auto-close) and the customer
      responds, the stage reverts to Pending Access. This signals the tech must
      review the new customer response and take action.

      AUDIT EVALUATION:
      - Identify if Pending Access appears AFTER Pending Complete in the stage history.
      - If yes: calculate the time between the Pending Access re-open timestamp
        and the next stage change timestamp.
      - This duration = ticket review turnaround time after customer re-engagement.
      - Identify WHICH individual was responsible at that point in time and attribute
        the delay (if any) to that person specifically.
      - Flag if the review turnaround is unreasonably long (treat with same
        urgency as first touch — target review within a reasonable window).
      - After reviewing, the responsible tech must move the ticket to the correct next stage:
          → If customer confirms issue persists: re-engage carrier, move to
            Pending Carrier Action.
          → If issue is resolved: move to Customer Confirming Resolution or
            back to Pending Complete.
          → If no further action needed: proceed to Completed.
      - Leaving the ticket in Pending Access without updating the stage after
        a customer response = process gap. Flag it against the responsible individual.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── AUTO CLOSE TIMER ───────────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="Auto Close Timer">
      Source: "How to set ticket to Auto close in iPath" — last updated May 18, 2026.
      - When moving to "Pending Complete", the Auto Close Timer field must be set (6–72 hours).
      - After saving to Pending Complete, a resolution code must be selected.
      - Auto-closure via timer expiry is a valid and fully equivalent closure method.
      - Do NOT penalize auto-timer closure vs manual closure — they are the same.
      - If a customer responds before the timer expires, the stage reverts to
        Pending Access and the auto-close timer is effectively paused/reset.
        The tech responsible at that time must re-evaluate and update the stage accordingly.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── FIRST TOUCH & CARRIER TICKET TIMING ───────────────── -->
    <standard name="First Touch Time">
      Source: April 2026 Service & Support Review (confirmed operational target).
      - 95% of tickets must be touched within 5 minutes of opening.
      - Average first touch target: 2 minutes.
      - Attribute first touch timing to whoever sent the first customer-facing response.
      - If a Contributor sent the first touch, deduct from Contributor — not Owner.
      - If Owner sent the first touch late, deduct from Owner — Contributor gets full score.
    </standard>

    <standard name="Carrier Ticket Opening">
      Source: April 2026 Service & Support Review (confirmed operational target).
      - Carrier ticket must be opened within 15 minutes of ticket creation on 92%+ of tickets.
      - eBonding (APEX API) is the preferred method for AT&T and supported carriers.
      - A carrier ticket NUMBER is NOT mandatory. Some carriers do not create formal
        tickets but instead provide verbal or written updates directly.
      - Do NOT penalize for a missing carrier ticket number. What matters is that
        the carrier was engaged and updates are documented in the ticket notes.
      - Evaluate carrier engagement based on evidence of contact and updates,
        not solely on whether a ticket number is present.
      - Attribute carrier engagement timing to whoever performed the carrier contact.
      - If a Contributor engaged the carrier, that timing is evaluated against
        the Contributor — not the Owner.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── MNS TICKETS AND ALERTS ─────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="MNS Tickets">
      Source: "MNS Tickets and Alerts" page — last updated May 24, 2026.
      - MNS = Managed Network System. Monitors customer data services via LogicMonitor.
      - MNS tickets are auto-created by the system.
      - Initial customer notification is auto-sent by the system upon ticket creation.
      - This allows NOC to begin troubleshooting before the customer is aware of the issue.
      - MNS Standard: alerts trigger after 10+ minutes of downtime.
        → Engage carrier immediately when ticket triggers (no delay).
      - MNS CPM / Hardware Management: alerts trigger after 3+ minutes of downtime.
        → Follow the circuit grade/downtime matrix (see RFO standard below).
      - For DHCP circuits: monitoring uses FQDN/hostname. No historical circuit data
        available from AT&T broadband.
      - Next Step field for MNS tickets should reflect current status
        (e.g., "outage - no ETR", "RFO - tech repaired line").
    </standard>

    <!-- ── RFO — ENGAGING CARRIERS ON MNS TICKETS ────────────── -->
    <standard name="RFO — Engaging Carriers on MNS Tickets">
      Source: "RFO-Engaging Carriers on MNS Tickets" page — last updated Apr 27, 2026.

      MNS Standard:
      - Alerts trigger after 10+ minutes of downtime.
      - No change in process — engage the carrier as soon as a ticket is triggered.

      MNS CPM / Hardware Management:
      - Alerts trigger after 3+ minutes of downtime.
      - Follow the circuit grade/downtime matrix below:

      Circuit Grade A:
      - Any downtime duration, any prior ticket count → Engage carrier immediately.

      Circuit Grade B/C:
      - Follow downtime duration and prior ticket count (60-day window) matrix before engaging.
      - If downtime is short and no prior tickets in 60 days → monitor before engaging.
      - If prior tickets exist within 60 days → engage carrier sooner per matrix thresholds.

      RFO Documentation:
      - RFO (Reason For Outage) must be documented.
      - Acceptable location: Next Step field OR ticket notes.
      - RFO documentation does NOT need to be written in full sentences or formal language.
        Abbreviations, shorthand, and codes are fully acceptable as long as they convey
        the reason for the outage.
        Examples of valid RFO entries:
          → "OU" = Over Utilization
          → "carrier outage - no ETR"
          → "RFO - tech repaired line"
          → "fiber cut"
          → "power issue on site"
          → Any similar shorthand that identifies the cause — accept it as valid RFO.
      - Do NOT penalize abbreviated or shorthand RFO entries. If there is any indication
        of a reason for the outage in the Next Step field or notes, treat it as RFO documented.
      - The Root Cause field in iPath has known functional issues.
        Do NOT penalize if Root Cause field is blank — it is a system limitation.
      - Root cause documented in Next Step field = fully acceptable, NOT a deduction.
      - Attribute RFO documentation to whoever documented it. If a Contributor documented
        the RFO, the Owner is not penalized for RFO documentation.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── LCON DETAILS — SCORING RULE ───────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="LCON Details">
      LCON (Local Contact) details scoring must follow these exact rules:

      DEDUCT score ONLY in these two specific scenarios:
        1. The customer provided LCON details (name, phone, email) and those details
           are NOT captured anywhere in the ticket.
        2. The carrier scheduled a dispatch to the customer site AND the tech did not
           capture the dispatch details (date, time, technician info) AND did not
           request those details from the customer.

      Do NOT deduct score in any other LCON-related scenario, including:
        - LCON details were not provided by the customer and are not in the ticket.
        - LCON name or email is blank but LCON phone is present.
        - No dispatch was scheduled, so no dispatch details are needed.
        - LCON fields are partially filled but the available information is captured.
        - Customer did not supply LCON information at all.

      ATTRIBUTION: Attribute LCON deductions to the individual who was responsible
      for capturing or requesting that information at that point in the ticket timeline.
      Do not penalize other individuals who were not involved in that interaction.

      RULE: Only penalize when there is clear evidence that information was available
      or required and the responsible tech failed to capture or request it. If the
      information was simply never provided or not applicable, do NOT penalize.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── REQUIRED FIELDS — SCORING EXCEPTIONS ──────────────── -->
    <standard name="Required Fields — Scoring Exceptions">
      The following fields are known system limitations or non-mandatory in practice.
      Do NOT penalize for any of these being blank:
      - Root Cause field: NOT functional in iPath.
      - MTTR field: system-calculated, not technician-controlled.
      - Carrier ticket number: NOT mandatory — some carriers do not create tickets
        but provide updates directly. Evaluate carrier engagement by evidence of
        contact and documented updates, not by presence of a ticket number.
      - "Is this the first time using the service?"
      - "Have there been any changes?"
      - "Power and cabling verified?"
      - "If carrier finds no trouble — billable dispatch approved?"
      - "Preferred Contact Method?"
      - Root cause documented in Next Step field instead of Root Cause field — fully acceptable.
      - RFO in abbreviated or shorthand form (e.g., "OU" for Over Utilization) — fully acceptable.
      LCON Name and LCON Email: evaluate per the LCON Details standard above.
      Only deduct if customer provided LCON details not captured in the ticket,
      or if a dispatch was scheduled and details were not captured or requested.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── SLA ESCALATION TIMERS ──────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="SLA Escalation Timers">
      Automated iPath escalation chain when ticket remains in Pending Carrier Action:
      - Lead Alert:  2+ hours → escalate to Lead.
      - MGR Alert:   3+ hours → escalate to Manager.
      - VP Alert:    4+ hours → escalate to VP.
      - SVP Alert:   5+ hours → escalate to SVP.
      Evaluate whether these triggered correctly based on ticket timestamps.
      Attribute escalation compliance or failure to the individual who was
      responsible for the ticket at the time the threshold was crossed.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── TSP CODES ──────────────────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="TSP Codes">
      TSP (Telecommunications Service Priority) codes apply ONLY under BOTH conditions:
        1. Customer = Stanford Health Care
        2. Service Type = DIA, VPLS, MPLS, or Point to Point

      If BOTH conditions are met:
        → TSP codes must be checked and referenced when opening a carrier ticket.
        → Failure to reference TSP codes = process gap, flag it.
        → Attribute this to whoever opened the carrier ticket.

      For ALL other customers: TSP codes are NOT applicable. Do NOT penalize.
      For Stanford Health Care with a service type outside DIA/VPLS/MPLS/P2P:
        → NOT applicable. Do NOT penalize.
      If service type is unclear: Do NOT penalize — note the assumption.
    </standard>

    <!-- ── DISPATCH SCHEDULING ────────────────────────────────── -->
    <standard name="Dispatch Scheduling">
      - When scheduling a technician dispatch, the customer's stated access hours
        and preferences must be respected.
      - If the customer specifies a preferred time window, that preference must be
        communicated to the carrier at first contact.
      - If the carrier schedules a dispatch, the tech must capture the dispatch
        details (date, time, technician info) in the ticket AND notify / confirm
        with the customer. Failure to do so = process gap. Flag it.
      - Failure to schedule within the customer's preferred window that requires
        additional escalation = process gap.
      - Attribute dispatch-related findings to whoever handled the dispatch interaction.
    </standard>
